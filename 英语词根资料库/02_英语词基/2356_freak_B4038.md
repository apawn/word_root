## 核心含义与来源

- 核心含义：n. 畸形人, 畸形物, 不正常的事物, 反复无常；a. 奇异的, 反常的
- 英文含义：Someone or something that is markedly unusual or unpredictable.
- 词形：freak

**多个词源／构词路径**：同一拼写可能有不同来源；以下按词性和含义分别列出。

**来源记录 1**（名词；Someone or something that is markedly unusual or unpredictable.）

First attested in 1567. The sense "sudden change of mind, a whim" is of uncertain origin. Probably from a dialectal word related to Middle English frekynge (“capricious behavior; whims”) and Middle English friken, frikien (“to move briskly or nimbly”), from Old English frīcian (“to leap, dance”), or Middle English frek (“insolent, daring”), from Old English frec (“desirous, greedy, eager, bold, daring”), from Proto-West Germanic \*frek, from Proto-Germanic \*frekaz, \*frakaz (“hard, efficient, greedy, bold, audacious”) (in which case, it would be related to the noun under etymology 3). Compare Old High German freh (“eager”), Old English frēcne (“dangerous”). For the meaning development compare Russian заско́к (zaskók) akin to ско́к (skók), скака́ть (skakátʹ).

**来源记录 2**（名词；A spot or streak of colour.）

The verb is a variant of freck. The noun is derived from the verb.

**来源记录 3**（名词；A man, particularly a bold, strong, vigorous man.）

From Middle English freke, freike (“a bold man, warrior, man, creature”), from Old English freca (“a bold man, warrior, hero”), from Proto-Germanic \*frekô (“an active or eager man, warrior, wolf”), from \*frekaz (“active, bold, desirous, greedy”), from Proto-Indo-European \*pereg-, \*spereg- (“to shrug, be quick, twitch, splash, blast”). Cognate with Old Norse freki (“greedy or avaricious one, a wolf”), Old High German freh (“eager”), German frech, Old English frēcne (“dangerous, daring, courageous, bold”).

## 逐词详解

<a id="w-1"></a>

### 1. freak

**词性**：名词　｜　**音标**：英 /fɹiːk/；美 /fɹik/

**中文释义**：n. 畸形人, 畸形物, 不正常的事物, 反复无常；a. 奇异的, 反常的

**英文义项**：Someone or something that is markedly unusual or unpredictable. / A person who is extremely abnormal in appearance, social behavior, sexual orientation, gender identity, or business practices; an oddball, a unique person, originally in a displeasing or alienating way.

**词源与演化**：

First attested in 1567. The sense "sudden change of mind, a whim" is of uncertain origin. Probably from a dialectal word related to Middle English frekynge (“capricious behavior; whims”) and Middle English friken, frikien (“to move briskly or nimbly”), from Old English frīcian (“to leap, dance”), or Middle English frek (“insolent, daring”), from Old English frec (“desirous, greedy, eager, bold, daring”), from Proto-West Germanic \*frek, from Proto-Germanic \*frekaz, \*frakaz (“hard, efficient, greedy, bold, audacious”) (in which case, it would be related to the noun under etymology 3). Compare Old High German freh (“eager”), Old English frēcne (“dangerous”). For the meaning development compare Russian заско́к (zaskók) akin to ско́к (skók), скака́ть (skakátʹ).

**例句**：

> Bob's a real video-game freak. He owns every games console of the last ten years.

该用例对应义项：An enthusiast, or person who has an obsession with, or extreme knowledge of, something.

<a id="w-2"></a>

### 2. freaky

**词性**：形容词　｜　**音标**：/ˈfɹiː.ki/

**中文释义**：a. (=freakish)畸形的, 捉摸不定的

**英文义项**：Resembling a freak. / Odd; bizarre; unusual.

**构词拆分**：freak + -y

**词源与演化**：

From freak + -y.

**例句**：

> I've heard the props and costumes in this play are quite freaky.

该用例对应义项：Odd; bizarre; unusual.

<a id="w-3"></a>

### 3. freakish

**词性**：形容词　｜　**音标**：/ˈfɹiːkɪʃ/

**中文释义**：a. 朝三暮四的, 见异思迁的, 畸形的

**英文义项**：Resembling a freak. / Strange, unusual, abnormal or bizarre.

**构词拆分**：freak + -ish

**词源与演化**：

From freak + -ish.

**例句**：

> What freakish weather we are having lately in these parts.

该用例对应义项：Capricious, unpredictable.

<a id="w-4"></a>

### 4. freakout

**词性**：名词　｜　**音标**：美 /ˈfɹikˌaʊt/

**中文释义**：n. 迷幻状态, 嬉皮士聚会, 逃避现实, 逃避现实者

**英文义项**：A frightening or disorientating experience, especially one that results from the use of a hallucinogenic drug. / An occurrence of unrestrained or irrational behaviour.

**构词拆分**：freak + out

**词源与演化**：

From freak + out, as a deverbal from freak out.
